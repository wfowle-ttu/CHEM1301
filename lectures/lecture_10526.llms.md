# How to Predict Precipitates When Solutions of Two Ionic Compounds Are Mixed

If something in our mixture is insoluble it will be visible at the bottom of the solution. That portion of the mixture is the precipitate.

1.  Write the reactants as they actually exist before any reaction occurs.
    - When a salt dissolves it’s ions seperate
2.  Consider the various solids that could form.
    - Swap the anions of the added salts
3.  Use the solubility rules table to determine whether a solid forms and if so predict the identiy of that solid.

## Net Ionic Equations

The formula is cancelled out to reveal what ions actually traded partners.

### Spectator Ions

Spectator Ions do not trade partners in a reaction and therefore are referred to as such.

## Types of Equations for REactions in Aqueous Solutions

1.  The molecular equation shows the overall reaction but doesn’t necessarily reveal the actual forms of the reactants and products in the solution.

2.  The complete ionic equation represents all reactants and products that are strong electrolytes as ions. All reactants and products are included.

3.  The net ionic equation includes only those components that undergo a change. Spectator ions are not included.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

To form all 3 equations start with the balanced molecular equation. Seperate each compound into it’s revelant Cations and Anions, that forms the complete ionic equation.

From there evaluate the state of matter of each product and find which ions have combined to form solids. Ions that remain aqueous are spectator ions and can be dropped from the equation.

The remaining compounds form the net ionic equation.

Complete: NaCl (aq) + Ag(NO_3) (aq) -\> AgCl (s) + NaNO_3 (aq)

Complete Ionic: Na^+ (aq) + Cl^- (aq) + Ag^+ (aq) + NO_3^- (aq) -\> AgCl(s) + Na^+ (aq) + NO_3^- (aq)

Net Ionic: Cl^- (aq) + Ag^+ (aq) -\> AgCl (s)

## Arrhenius Acids

Svante Arrhenius proposed that an acid is a substance that produces H^+ ions when it is dissolved with water.

When HCl, HNO_3 and H_2SO_4 are placed in water virtually every molecule disassociates the give ions.

Because these substances are strong electrolytes that produce H^+ ions they are called strong acids.

Weak acids therefore are not as efficient as electrolytes.

## Arrhenius Bases

Arrhenius also found that aquesous solutions that exhibit basic behavior always contain hydroxide ions.

He defined a base as a substance that produces hydroxide ions (OH^-) in water.

Hydroxide compounds that are strong electrolytes are called strong bases.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Acid-Base Reactions

Acids and Bases react together to form water.

The result of a reaction between an acid and a base is water (H_2O) and another ionic compound called a salt, which may or may not form a precipitate.

Strong Acids and Strong Bases completely disassociate in water.

There are seven strong acids to remember:

HCl

HBr

HI

HClO_4

HNO_3

H_2SO_4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Six strong bases are the same bases that are soluble.

The net ionic equation for the reaction of strong acid and a strong base is always the same. It shows the production of water.
